Assessment of the SedimentDrift resuspension physics after the M1 campaign
==========================================================================
Written 2026-08-31 by Claude (Opus 5), at S. Soares' request, in answer to:
"is the new code physically sound, is the resuspension behavior realistic and
within expectations?"

This is a judgement, not a test result. The measurements it rests on are in
validation/REPORT.md and the notebooks in validation/notebooks/; the one new
measurement made while answering (the timestep-convergence sweep) is recorded
in section 4 below and has since been added to REPORT.md as F3b.


VERDICT, IN TWO PARTS
---------------------
The supporting machinery is physically sound. The resuspension scheme is not.

Those two are separable, and it matters that they are: everything that FEEDS
resuspension -- settling velocity, vertical mixing, bed shear stress -- is
validated against external references, so the defect is localised to the
erosion step itself rather than being diffused through the model.


1. WHAT IS SOLID
----------------
Each of these was checked against an external reference (published data, an
analytic limit, or a published model result), not against the model's own
self-consistency.

  settling closures      log10-RMSE 0.20 against Maggi (2013) MEASURED grains;
                         Stokes low-Re limit exact across three closures; the
                         BB16 shape correction measurably improves the fit
                         (0.219 -> 0.202)

  vertical mixing        well-mixed condition holds to 10 % across the top 30 %
                         of the column, where K falls by 33,600x. This is the
                         region where a random walk missing the dK/dz term
                         piles particles against the boundary; it does not

  settling (x) mixing    Rouse exponent recovered within tolerance and the
                         modelled profile tracks the analytic shape over two
                         decades of concentration

  bed shear stress       SG2000 kernel reproduces the published UserTestCase
                         Table 2 to max abs error 0.000 (15 entries)

  deposition             0.719 settled vs analytic w_s t / h = 0.720

  class behaviour        Sherwood (2018) 3.2.1: suspended-load ordering by
                         grain size, deposition sequence (140 -> 62.5 -> 30 um,
                         4 um never), and retention of the finest class at
                         day 5 all reproduced

The last row is the important one for the science. The model gets the
QUALITATIVE sediment behaviour right: coarse deposits first, fines stay up, a
graded deposit forms. The physics of which class does what is correct.


2. WHERE IT BREAKS
------------------
Resuspension is implemented as a PER-PARTICLE BINARY STATE CHANGE, where the
real process is a MASS FLUX. Every settled element whose critical stress is
exceeded is lifted, on every timestep, regardless of by how much. Three
consequences follow, all from that single root cause:

  (a) No intensity scaling  (F2)
      A 0.45 Pa event resuspends 0.92x as much sand as a 1.00 Pa event.
      Sherwood reports "minimal amounts" for the weaker event, because his
      erosion is a flux proportional to the excess stress. A threshold
      crossing has no notion of HOW FAR it was exceeded.

  (b) The eroded amount is a numerical choice, not a physical one
      Everything above threshold lifts, so the mass mobilised is set by how
      many numerical particles happen to be resting on the bed. There is no
      erodible-mass or active-layer constraint anywhere in the scheme.

  (c) The rate does not converge  (F3)
      See section 4. This is the decisive one.


3. THE MIXED-BED THRESHOLD IS SEPARATELY WRONG  (F1)
----------------------------------------------------
Independent of the flux problem. Sherwood Eq. 6 blends the particle's own
Shields stress toward the BULK BED stress, one value for every size class, and
floors the result at the particle's own tau_c:

    tau_ce = max[ Pc*tau_cb + (1-Pc)*tau_c , tau_c ]

Ours blends toward a fractal floc strength computed from the PARTICLE'S OWN
diameter, and omits the floor. A sand grain embedded in a mud matrix is
therefore assigned the fictitious strength of a 140 um floc. Errors run 0.6x
(clay, too easy to erode, because the floor is missing) to 14x (coarse sand,
far too hard). The low-mud limit is exact, and the monotonicity in mud content
is right, so the structure is sound and the cohesive limit is not.


4. THE DECISIVE MEASUREMENT: THE SCHEME DOES NOT CONVERGE
---------------------------------------------------------
Made 2026-08-31 while writing this assessment. One class (140 um sand,
tau_ce = 0.10 Pa, w_s = 8 mm/s), one half-sine stress event peaking at 1.0 Pa
and spending 23.0 h above threshold, 200 particles. ONLY the outer timestep
was varied:

    dt [s]      steps above threshold    mean lift-offs    lift-offs/step
    900                            92              67.5              0.73
    450                           184              91.7              0.50
    225                           368             110.3              0.30

Refining dt by 4x changes the answer by +63 %, monotonically, with no sign of
settling to a limit. A physical quantity must converge as dt -> 0. This one
does not: the lift-off count is set by how often resuspension() happens to be
called.

The lift-offs/step ratio falls because a smaller step gives the particle more
steps in which to fall back; the ABSOLUTE count nevertheless keeps rising.

This matters beyond bookkeeping. Each lift-off lofts a grain into faster-moving
water, where it is advected before settling back, so the resulting HORIZONTAL
TRANSPORT inherits the same timestep dependence.

This upgrades F3 from "too much resuspension" to "not a convergent scheme". A
miscalibrated threshold would still be physical, with a wrong constant. This is
not that.


5. IS THE BEHAVIOUR "WITHIN EXPECTATIONS"?
------------------------------------------
No -- and specifically, it is worse than being merely too energetic.

The practical picture is BIMODAL, with no well-behaved middle regime:

  tau_crit ABOVE the ambient stress
      Resuspension never fires. The run is effectively pure settling plus
      advection. Defensible as a modelling choice, but the resuspension
      physics is then inert and contributes nothing. This is what the bb16
      production run did: 23 events over 19 particles. Under the production
      default tau_crit_mode='auto', 31 um quartz falls below the 63 um cutoff,
      is routed to the cohesive branch, and receives ~0.48 Pa against a legacy
      prescribed value of 0.09 Pa.

  tau_crit BELOW the ambient stress
      Pathological hopping, as measured above. This is the mechanism behind
      the ~45 % out-of-domain loss of the fastest settling classes that
      currently blocks the class-weight inversion. THAT NUMBER IS A NUMERICAL
      ARTIFACT, NOT A PHYSICAL EXPORT FLUX, and it would change if the
      timestep changed.

Anything sitting near the threshold inherits the worst of both.


6. WHAT I WOULD AND WOULD NOT USE THIS CODE FOR TODAY
-----------------------------------------------------
  TRUST      settling, dispersal, deposition patterns, and the relative
             behaviour of different particle classes. That chain is validated
             against published measurements and analytic limits.

  DO NOT     resuspension event counts; resuspension-driven export or domain
  TRUST      loss; any erosion-intensity or storm-to-storm comparison; and
             'mixed'-mode critical stresses (F1).

Also note F6: times_resuspended was declared uint8 and wrapped silently at 255.
It is in the export_variables list of the production runs, so resuspension
counts in EXISTING output are corrupted wherever a particle exceeded 255
lift-offs -- which, given section 4, the fast classes certainly did. Fixed to
uint32; deposition and position fields are unaffected.


7. THE FIX IS WELL-DEFINED
--------------------------
Replace the threshold crossing with an Ariathurai-Partheniades pickup flux,

    E = E_0 (1 - phi) (tau_b / tau_c - 1)      [kg m-2 s-1]

so erosion becomes a RATE with a mass constraint. That addresses F2 and F3
together: it is intensity-scaled by construction, and the expected mass eroded
per unit time is independent of dt, which is what makes it convergent. The stub
(calc_upward_resuspension_velocity) already exists and now works, though its
normalisation (tau - tau_c)/tau differs from the ROMS/COAWST form used above
and that discrepancy is unresolved.

The non-obvious prerequisite is that this needs a notion of ERODIBLE MASS PER
UNIT BED AREA, which a Lagrangian model does not currently have: elements carry
no mass at all. That is the real design work, and it is why the fix is a module
rather than a patch.

F1 and F4 (no critical shear stress for deposition, Krone tau_d) are
independent, smaller, and also M2.


8. WAS THE CAMPAIGN WORTH RUNNING?
----------------------------------
Yes. Three of these defects had been live in the PRODUCTION DEFAULTS since
2026-06-19, and the diagnostic that would have exposed the hopping
(times_resuspended) was itself silently broken by an 8-bit overflow. None of it
was visible from the model's own outputs; all of it became visible within a day
of comparing against published cases with known answers.
